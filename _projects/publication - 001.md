	<h3>Abstract</h3>
	<p>With the rapid growth of the consumption of new energy vehicles, the amount of waste lithium-ion batteries 
(LIBs) has surged exponentially. These spent LIBs contain valuable metals such as lithium, cobalt, nickel, and 
manganese, offering significant recycling value while simultaneously imposing substantial environmental pressures. At present, a bottleneck in the recycling process is achieving a balance between cleanliness and efficiency. 
Consequently, developing clean and efficient recycling technologies is crucial for safeguarding strategic resource 
security and achieving environmental sustainability. Addressing the substantial research output in this field over 
recent years, this paper provides a systematic review of the latest advances in waste LIBs recycling processes, 
with particular focus on carbon emissions across different technological pathways. The paper examines the 
technical principles and current status of physical methods, chemical methods (including hydrometallurgy and 
pyrometallurgy), and combined recycling processes. From a sustainability perspective, this review provides a 
boundary-aware multidimensional assessment of representative recycling routes by considering carbon emissions, energy and chemical consumption, secondary environmental burdens, product quality, process complexity, 
technological maturity, and techno-economic feasibility. On this basis, the review identifies the key trade-offs 
and future directions for moving from single-unit operation optimization toward integrated, short-process, 
and reagent-efficient recycling systems that better balance resource recovery, product quality, technoeconomic feasibility, and low-carbon performance.</p>

	<h3>Keywords</h3>
